## 1. Key Terms

| Term | Short definition (handout wording, simplified) | Memory hook |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gameplay** | Choices, challenges, or consequences players face while navigating a virtual environment, linked with story structure and character development like plot points; what truly makes a game compelling. | **C-C-C** (Choices, Challenges, Consequences) = the "plot points" of playing. |
| **Rules** | Define the actions/moves players may and may not make; communicated via in-game tutorials, hints, or an instruction manual. | What you **can and cannot** do. |
| **Victory Conditions** | Correspond to how players win the game (1 winner, several, or many) and the exact point where winning is met (e.g., destroying the enemy's base in Mobile Legends). | **Win point** (how & when you win). |
| **Loss Conditions** | Specify how players lose the game; divided into implicit and explicit types. | **Lose point** (how you lose). |
| **Implicit Loss Condition** | Common in games requiring competition between the player and other players or NPCs (e.g., racing game). | **I**mplicit = **I**n competition (losing a race to others). |
| **Explicit Loss Condition** | Player loses because their character dies or runs out of vital resources; common in construction and management games. | **E**xplicit = **E**mpty resources or character death. |
| **Interactivity Modes** | Types of interactivity affecting gameplay where the interactive element originates with the player, showing the importance of player decisions. | Player-driven **connections** (to Game, Player, or Dev). |
| **Player-to-Game** | Very common in single-player mode; player interacts only with the game itself and the platform, where NPCs are generated by AI. | **1-on-1 with AI** and the platform. |
| **Player-to-Player** | Multiplayer mode where players interact with the game and each other—how they communicate and play together (cooperatively and/or competitively). | **Co-op or PvP** between human players. |
| **Player-to-Developer** | Players interacting with the actual game developers, commonly in chat rooms and discussion forums on the game's website. | **Forums & chat rooms** with the makers. |
| **Game Theory** | Focuses on types of conflicts in games and how players respond to them; applies to games with two or more opponents. | **Conflict math** for 2+ opponents. |
| **Zero-Sum (ZS)** | Situations where players have completely opposing interests and only one winner is possible (e.g., chess). | **1 winner, 1 loser** (+1 - 1 = 0). |
| **Non-Zero Sum (NZS)** | Situations where players do not have completely opposing interests; common in MMOs where players form teams/guilds to cooperate against NPCs. | **Team up** against common NPC enemies. |
| **Prisoner's Dilemma** | Happens when all players try to compete with each other in an NZS situation (e.g., battle royale where everyone must eliminate everyone else to survive). | **Everyone-for-themselves** inside an NZS setup (Battle Royale). |
| **Challenges** | Series of linked challenges in gameplay, often tied to the game's genre so genre fans expect certain challenges. | Genre-linked **obstacles** to overcome. |
| **Explicit Challenge** | A challenge that is intentional, immediate, and often intense. | **On-purpose & in-your-face** right now. |
| **Implicit Challenge** | A challenge not specifically added to the game, but an emergent feature of the game itself. | **Emergent** side effect of the game's systems. |
| **Perfect Information** | The complete state of play is known to players at all times; yields logical challenges where players assimilate info to pick the best action. | **Full visibility** = pure logic. |
| **Imperfect Information** | Players get only a fraction of the info needed for the best decision; requires **inference** (guessing the nature of missing info). | **Partial visibility** = requires guessing (**inference**). |
| **Inference** | The ability to make a guess about the nature of missing information in imperfect-information games. | **Educated guess** for hidden info. |
| **Intrinsic Knowledge** | Knowledge gained from **within** the game world (e.g., learning a magical machine's purpose from an alchemist NPC). | **In**trinsic = **In**side the game world. |
| **Extrinsic Knowledge** | Knowledge gained **outside** the game world and applied to the game (e.g., knowing Water Pokémon beats Fire Pokémon). | **Ex**trinsic = **Ex**ternal (real-world/prior) knowledge. |
| **Spatial Awareness** | Challenges where players navigate through environments; very common in puzzle games (e.g., *Monument Valley*). | **Navigating spaces** & paths. |
| **Resource Management** | Managing settings and actions of resources/characters; called **micromanagement** in high-detail games to give players many options (e.g., *Clash of Clans*). | **Managing supplies/units** (high detail = **micromanagement**). |
| **Reaction Time** | Action-game challenge where player response speed directly controls character reaction speed; slow reaction means missed opportunities or death. | **Fast reflexes** = life or death. |
| **Advancement** | *(Game Goal)* Reaching a higher level; successive levels may increase difficulty (arcade/puzzle) or "leveling up" makes a character stronger (RPGs). | **Leveling up** or next stage. |
| **Race** | *(Game Goal)* Accomplishing something before another player does; a reaction-time challenge in action and multiplayer puzzle games. | **First to finish** wins. |
| **Analysis** | *(Game Goal)* Applying mental processes to solve riddles and cryptic codes; involves almost every other challenge type, wide in puzzle/adventure games. | **Cracking codes & riddles**. |
| **Exploration** | *(Game Goal)* Moving into new areas and seeing new things; satisfies player curiosity in adventure games and RPGs. | **Discovering new places**. |
| **Conflict** | *(Game Goal)* Disagreements or combat between characters; used in almost all genres for dramatic tension. | **Fighting / clashing** for drama. |
| **Capture** | *(Game Goal)* Taking or destroying something belonging to an opponent without being captured or killed in return; overarching goal across genres (action, RTS). | **Take/destroy theirs** safely. |
| **Chase** | *(Game Goal)* Catching or eluding an opponent using quick reflexes or stealth strategies; popular in action and stealth games. | **Tag / pursuit / evasion**. |
| **Organization** | *(Game Goal)* Arranging items in a particular order using spatial and pattern-matching strategies; common in puzzle (*Bejeweled*, *Tetris*) and resource strategy games. | **Sorting & lining up** patterns. |
| **Escape** | *(Game Goal)* Rescuing items or players and taking them to safety; often uses analytical reasoning and resource management. | **Rescue to safety**. |
| **Taboo** | *(Game Goal)* Getting the competition to "break the rules"; often involves physical or emotional stamina (e.g., *Twister*, *Don't Break the Ice*). | **Force a foul** on the opponent. |
| **Construction** | *(Game Goal)* Building and maintaining objects; common in process simulations involving resource management and trade. | **Build & maintain** things. |
| **Solution** | *(Game Goal)* Solving a problem or puzzle before or more accurately than the competition; uses analytical reasoning and knowledge, common in detective adventure games. | **Solve first / most accurately** (detective work). |
| **Outwit** | *(Game Goal)* Applying intrinsic or extrinsic knowledge to defeat the competition. | **Outsmart** using knowledge. |
| **Balance** | Perceived by players as consistent, fair, and fun; ease of winning grows with skill, and a better player beats a less-skilled player (unless purely luck-based). | **Consistent, fair, fun**; skill beats luck. |
| **Consistent challenges** | *(Balance Requirement)* Players experience gradually more difficult challenges. | **Smooth difficulty curve**. |
| **Perceivably fair playing experiences** | *(Balance Requirement)* Players shouldn't be doomed from the start through their "mistakes." | **No instant-loss traps** from early mistakes. |
| **Lack of stagnation** | *(Balance Requirement)* Players should never get stuck with no way to go on. | **Never soft-locked**. |
| **Lack of trivial decisions** | *(Balance Requirement)* Players should only make important decisions, even in micromanagement games. | **No pointless busywork** choices. |
| **Difficulty levels** | *(Balance Requirement)* Players have a choice of difficulty, or the level adjusts to the player's ability throughout the game. | **Selectable or adaptive** difficulty. |

## 2. Look-Alike / Confusing Terms

### Group 1: Explicit vs. Implicit (Loss Conditions vs. Challenges)
| Term | What it is | Key difference | Clue words in a question |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Explicit Loss Condition** | How you lose by character death or running out of vital resources | **Loss condition** tied to death/resources (construction & management games) | "character dies", "runs out of vital resources", "construction/management" |
| **Implicit Loss Condition** | How you lose in competitive play against players or NPCs | **Loss condition** tied to losing a competition (e.g., racing) | "competition", "other players or NPCs", "racing game" |
| **Explicit Challenge** | Intentional, immediate, and often intense obstacle | **Challenge** deliberately designed into the game | "intentional", "immediate", "intense" |
| **Implicit Challenge** | Unplanned obstacle emerging naturally from game systems | **Challenge** that is an emergent feature, not specifically added | "not specifically added", "emergent feature" |

> **Trap:** Don't mix up *Explicit/Implicit Loss Conditions* (ways to lose) with *Explicit/Implicit Challenges* (obstacles during play)—check whether the scenario describes **losing the game** or **facing a challenge**.

### Group 2: Interactivity Modes
| Term | What it is | Key difference | Clue words in a question |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Player-to-Game** | Player interacts only with the game, platform, and AI NPCs | **No other human players**; single-player mode | "single-player", "NPCs", "AI system", "platform" |
| **Player-to-Player** | Players interact with the game and with each other | **Human-to-human** cooperation or competition | "multiplayer", "communicate with each other", "cooperative/competitive" |
| **Player-to-Developer** | Players interact with the people who made the game | Happens **outside** the match via forums/chat | "chat rooms", "discussion forums", "game's website", "developers" |

> **Trap:** Human-like NPCs still count as **Player-to-Game** (not Player-to-Player) because they are generated by an AI system.

### Group 3: Game Theory Conflicts
| Term | What it is | Key difference | Clue words in a question |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Zero-Sum (ZS)** | Completely opposing interests; only one winner possible | Impossible for both sides to get what they want | "completely opposing interests", "only one winner", "chess" |
| **Non-Zero Sum (NZS)** | Interests are not completely opposing; players can cooperate | Players team up/guild up against NPCs | "do not have completely opposing", "teams or guilds", "MMO", "cooperating" |
| **Prisoner's Dilemma** | All players compete with each other inside an NZS situation | Everyone vs. everyone for survival | "compete with each other in an NZS", "battle royale", "eliminate all other players" |

> **Trap:** *Prisoner's Dilemma* is specifically an **NZS situation** where everyone chooses to compete instead of cooperating (like a Battle Royale), NOT a standard Zero-Sum 1v1 like chess.

### Group 4: Information & Knowledge
| Term | What it is | Key difference | Clue words in a question |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Perfect Information** | Complete state of play known at all times | Yields **logical challenges**; no guessing | "complete state of play", "at all times", "logical challenges" |
| **Imperfect Information** | Only a fraction of info is provided | Requires **inference** (guessing missing info) | "fraction of the information", "inference", "missing information" |
| **Intrinsic Knowledge** | Info learned **inside** the game world | Taught by NPCs/game world (e.g., alchemist NPC) | "within the game world", "from an NPC", "magical machine" |
| **Extrinsic Knowledge** | Info learned **outside** the game world and applied to it | Prior/external knowledge (e.g., Pokémon type chart) | "outside the game world", "applied to the game", "Pokémon" |

> **Trap:** *Perfect/Imperfect Information* is about **how much of the game state is visible right now**, while *Intrinsic/Extrinsic Knowledge* is about **where you learned the rule/fact** (inside vs. outside the game).

### Group 5: Victory, Rules & Loss
| Term | What it is | Key difference | Clue words in a question |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rules** | Actions/moves players may and may not make | Defines **legal vs. illegal moves** | "actions or moves", "cannot make", "tutorials/hints/manual" |
| **Victory Conditions** | How players win and the point where winning is met | Defines **winning** (1, several, or many winners) | "how players should win", "destroying enemy's base" |
| **Loss Conditions** | How players lose the game | Defines **losing** (implicit or explicit) | "how players lose", "dies or runs out of resources" |

> **Trap:** Tutorials, hints, and instruction manuals communicate **Rules**, not Victory Conditions.

### Group 6: Mental & Knowledge Game Goals
| Term | What it is | Key difference | Clue words in a question |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Analysis** | Applying mental processes to solve riddles and cryptic codes | Involves almost every other challenge type; pure mental decoding | "riddles", "cryptic codes", "mental challenge" |
| **Solution** | Solving a problem/puzzle **before or more accurately than** competition | Competitive/detective problem solving | "before or more accurately than the competition", "detective work" |
| **Outwit** | Applying intrinsic or extrinsic knowledge to defeat competition | Winning specifically via **knowledge application** | "intrinsic or extrinsic knowledge", "defeat the competition" |

> **Trap:** *Solution* emphasizes beating the competition in speed/accuracy (detective work), whereas *Analysis* is the general mental challenge of riddles/codes, and *Outwit* is specifically using intrinsic/extrinsic knowledge.

### Group 7: Opponent-Focused Action Goals
| Term | What it is | Key difference | Clue words in a question |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Capture** | Taking/destroying an opponent's item without being captured/killed | Focuses on **taking/destroying an object** safely | "taking or destroying something", "without being captured or killed" |
| **Chase** | Catching or eluding an opponent | Focuses on **pursuit or evasion** (reflexes/stealth) | "catching or eluding", "quick reflexes or stealth" |
| **Escape** | Rescuing items or players and taking them to safety | Focuses on **rescuing to safety** (analysis + resources) | "rescuing items or players", "taking them to safety" |

> **Trap:** *Capture* takes/destroys the enemy's thing; *Escape* rescues your own/allied items or players to safety; *Chase* is catching or running from the opponent themselves.

### Group 8: Management, Layout & Building Goals
| Term | What it is | Key difference | Clue words in a question |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Resource Management** | Managing settings/actions of resources or characters | Challenge type; called **micromanagement** at high detail | "settings and actions", "micromanagement", "Clash of Clans" |
| **Organization** | Arranging items in a particular order | Game goal using **spatial & pattern-matching** (*Tetris*, *Bejeweled*) | "arranging items", "particular order", "pattern-matching" |
| **Construction** | Building and maintaining objects | Game goal in **process simulations** using resources & trade | "building and maintaining", "process simulations", "trade" |

> **Trap:** *Organization* is arranging/matching items in order (*Tetris*), while *Construction* is building and maintaining objects in simulations.

### Group 9: Balance System Requirements
| Term | What it is | Key difference | Clue words in a question |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Consistent challenges** | Gradually more difficult challenges | Smooth **progression** of difficulty | "gradually more difficult" |
| **Perceivably fair playing experiences** | Players aren't doomed from the start by early mistakes | No **unwinnable early traps** | "doomed from the start", "mistakes" |
| **Lack of stagnation** | Players never get stuck with no way to go on | No **dead-ends / soft-locks** | "never get stuck", "no way to go on" |
| **Lack of trivial decisions** | Players only make important decisions | Cuts **pointless micromanagement** choices | "only important decisions", "even in micromanagement" |
| **Difficulty levels** | Choice of difficulty or auto-adjusting level | **Selectable or adaptive** challenge | "choice of difficulty", "adjust to the player's ability" |

> **Trap:** *Perceivably fair* = not doomed from the start by mistakes; *Lack of stagnation* = never stuck with no way forward.

## 3. Likely Distractors

| Major Term | 3 Most Likely Distractors | How to Tell Them Apart (1-Line) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gameplay** | Rules, Balance, Challenges | **Gameplay** is the whole set of choices/challenges/consequences like plot points; **Rules** limit moves, **Challenges** are obstacles, **Balance** is fairness. |
| **Implicit Loss Condition** | Explicit Loss Condition, Implicit Challenge, Zero-Sum | **Implicit Loss** = losing in competition (racing), **Explicit Loss** = death/no resources, **Implicit Challenge** = emergent obstacle, **Zero-Sum** = 1-winner conflict. |
| **Player-to-Game** | Player-to-Player, Player-to-Developer, Non-Zero Sum | **Player-to-Game** = single-player vs. AI/platform; **Player-to-Player** = human multiplayer; **Player-to-Developer** = website forums/chats; **NZS** = team cooperation. |
| **Prisoner's Dilemma** | Zero-Sum, Non-Zero Sum, Conflict | **Prisoner's Dilemma** = everyone competes in an NZS game (Battle Royale); **ZS** = pure 1v1 opposition; **NZS** = cooperating teams; **Conflict** = dramatic tension goal. |
| **Imperfect Information** | Perfect Information, Inference, Extrinsic Knowledge | **Imperfect Info** = only partial game state is shown; **Perfect Info** = full state shown; **Inference** = the act of guessing; **Extrinsic** = outside-world knowledge. |
| **Intrinsic Knowledge** | Extrinsic Knowledge, Perfect Information, Analysis | **Intrinsic** = learned inside the game world (NPC); **Extrinsic** = learned outside the game; **Perfect Info** = full board state; **Analysis** = solving codes/riddles. |
| **Resource Management** | Micromanagement, Construction, Organization | **Resource Management** = managing resources/units; **Micromanagement** = high-detail version of it; **Construction** = building/maintaining objects; **Organization** = ordering patterns. |
| **Advancement** | Race, Exploration, Difficulty levels | **Advancement** = reaching higher levels/leveling up; **Race** = finishing first; **Exploration** = visiting new areas; **Difficulty levels** = selectable/adaptive balance. |
| **Capture** | Chase, Escape, Conflict | **Capture** = take/destroy enemy's thing safely; **Chase** = catch/elude opponent; **Escape** = rescue to safety; **Conflict** = combat/disagreement for tension. |
| **Solution** | Analysis, Outwit, Inference | **Solution** = solve puzzle faster/more accurately than rivals; **Analysis** = general riddle/code solving; **Outwit** = beat rivals using intrinsic/extrinsic knowledge; **Inference** = guessing hidden info. |
| **Taboo** | Rules, Conflict, Explicit Loss Condition | **Taboo** = goal of making rivals break the rules; **Rules** = allowed/disallowed moves; **Conflict** = combat/disagreements; **Explicit Loss** = death/empty resources. |
| **Lack of stagnation** | Perceivably fair, Lack of trivial decisions, Consistent challenges | **Lack of stagnation** = never stuck with no way forward; **Perceivably fair** = not doomed from start; **Lack of trivial decisions** = only important choices; **Consistent** = gradual difficulty. |

## 4. Lists, Sequences & "Which is NOT"

1. **2 Types of Loss Conditions**
   - Implicit loss condition (competition vs. players/NPCs, e.g., racing)

   - *Fake "NOT" swap-ins:* Strategic loss condition, Perfect loss condition, Taboo loss condition.

2. **3 Ways Rules Are Communicated**
   - In-game tutorials
   - Hints
   - Instruction manual
   - *Fake "NOT" swap-ins:* Discussion forums, Victory conditions, Balance patches.

3. **3 Interactivity Modes**
   - Player-to-Game
   - Player-to-Player
   - Player-to-Developer
   - *Fake "NOT" swap-ins:* Player-to-NPC (that's part of Player-to-Game), Developer-to-Platform, Game-to-Game.

4. **3 Game Theory Concepts / Situations**
   - Zero-Sum
   - Non-Zero Sum
   - Prisoner's Dilemma
   - *Fake "NOT" swap-ins:* Imperfect Information, Implicit Challenge, Taboo.

5. **9 Types of Challenges**
   1. Explicit
   2. Implicit
   3. Perfect Information
   4. Imperfect Information
   5. Intrinsic Knowledge
   6. Extrinsic Knowledge
   7. Spatial Awareness
   8. Resource Management (Micromanagement)
   9. Reaction Time
   - *Fake "NOT" swap-ins:* Advancement, Taboo, Construction (those are *Game Goals*, not Challenge types).

6. **13 Challenges & Game Goals**
   1. Advancement
   2. Race
   3. Analysis
   4. Exploration
   5. Conflict
   6. Capture
   7. Chase
   8. Organization
   9. Escape
   10. Taboo
   11. Construction
   12. Solution
   13. Outwit

## 6. Last-Minute Cram Sheet

- **Gameplay** = choices, challenges, and consequences linked like plot points with story and character development; it is what makes a game compelling.
- **Rules** define what moves players can and cannot make, taught via tutorials, hints, or instruction manuals.
- **Victory Conditions** define how and at what point you win (1, several, or many winners); **Loss Conditions** are **Implicit** (competition, e.g., racing) or **Explicit** (death or running out of vital resources).
- **Interactivity Modes** always originate with the player: **Player-to-Game** (single-player + AI NPCs), **Player-to-Player** (co-op/competitive multiplayer), and **Player-to-Developer** (website chat rooms/forums).
- **Game Theory** (2+ opponents): **Zero-Sum** = completely opposing interests, 1 winner (chess); **Non-Zero Sum** = can cooperate against NPCs (MMOs); **Prisoner's Dilemma** = everyone competes in an NZS setup (Battle Royale).
- **Explicit Challenges** are intentional and immediate; **Implicit Challenges** are emergent features not specifically added.
- **Perfect Information** = full state known (logical challenges); **Imperfect Information** = partial state known (requires **inference**).
- **Intrinsic Knowledge** comes from *inside* the game world (NPCs); **Extrinsic Knowledge** comes from *outside* the game world (e.g., Pokémon type matchups).
- **Micromanagement** is high-detail **Resource Management** (e.g., *Clash of Clans*) that gives players many options.
- **Mental/Knowledge Goals**: **Analysis** = riddles/codes; **Solution** = solve faster/more accurately than rivals (detective work); **Outwit** = beat rivals using intrinsic/extrinsic knowledge.
- **Action/Opponent Goals**: **Capture** = take/destroy enemy's thing safely; **Chase** = catch/elude opponent; **Escape** = rescue items/players to safety; **Taboo** = make rivals break the rules.
- **Spatial/Building Goals**: **Organization** = arrange items in order (*Tetris*, *Bejeweled*); **Construction** = build & maintain objects in process simulations.
- **Balance** means consistent, fair, and fun—where a more skilled player beats a less-skilled player unless the game is purely luck-based.
- **5 Balance Requirements**: Consistent challenges, Perceivably fair playing experiences (not doomed from the start), Lack of stagnation (never stuck), Lack of trivial decisions (only important choices), and Difficulty levels (selectable or adaptive).

## Answer Key

1. **B. Gameplay** — The handout defines gameplay as choices, challenges, or consequences linked with story and character development like plot points. *Rules* only define allowed/disallowed moves.
2. **B. Explicit loss condition** — Losing because a character dies or runs out of vital resources (common in construction/management games) is an explicit loss condition. *Implicit* loss is for competitive races against rivals.
3. **B. Implicit loss condition** — Losing through competition against other players or NPCs (such as a racing game) is an implicit loss condition. *Explicit* loss requires death or resource depletion.
4. **C. Player-to-Game** — Even when NPCs show human characteristics, they are AI-generated, so single-player interaction with them is Player-to-Game. *Player-to-Player* requires other human players.
5. **C. Player-to-Developer** — Chat rooms and discussion forums on the game's website where developers participate directly illustrate Player-to-Developer interactivity. *Player-to-Player* is between players.
6. **B. Zero-Sum** — Players have completely opposing interests and only one winner is possible, matching the handout's chess example. *Non-Zero Sum* allows cooperation.
7. **C. Non-Zero Sum** — MMO players forming teams or guilds to cooperate against NPCs do not have completely opposing interests. *Prisoner's Dilemma* occurs when everyone competes instead.
8. **B. Prisoner's Dilemma** — Battle royale is the handout's exact example of all players competing with each other in an NZS situation. *Zero-Sum* applies to pure opposing-interest games like chess.
9. **B. Perfect Information** — When the complete state of play is known at all times, it yields logical challenges. *Imperfect Information* hides part of the game state.
10. **B. Inference** — Imperfect information requires inference, defined as the ability to make a guess about missing information. *Perfect Information* uses logic instead of guessing.
11. **B. Intrinsic Knowledge** — Learning from an alchemist NPC inside the game world is the handout's example of intrinsic knowledge. *Extrinsic Knowledge* comes from outside the game.
12. **B. Extrinsic Knowledge** — Knowing Water beats Fire in Pokémon from outside the game world is the handout's example of extrinsic knowledge. *Intrinsic Knowledge* is learned inside the game.
13. **C. Micromanagement** — High-detail resource management (e.g., *Clash of Clans*) is called micromanagement. *Construction* is a game goal for building/maintaining objects.
14. **B. Reaction Time** — Action games test reaction time when player response speed directly affects whether the character catches a vanishing treasure. *Race* is a multiplayer goal of beating another player.
15. **D. Micromanagement** — Micromanagement is a form of Resource Management (a Challenge type), not one of the 13 Challenges & Game Goals. *Taboo*, *Outwit*, and *Escape* are all in the 13 goals.
16. **B. Organization** — Arranging items in a particular order using spatial/pattern-matching strategies (*Bejeweled*, *Tetris*) is Organization. *Construction* is building/maintaining objects in simulations.
17. **B. Solution** — Solving a problem or puzzle before or more accurately than the competition (common in detective adventure games) is Solution. *Analysis* is the general mental challenge of riddles/codes.
18. **C. Taboo** — Getting the competition to "break the rules" using physical or emotional stamina (*Twister*, *Don't Break the Ice*) is Taboo. *Conflict* is general combat/disagreement.
19. **C. Escape** — Rescuing items or players and taking them to safety using analytical reasoning and resource management is Escape. *Capture* is taking/destroying an opponent's property.
20. **B. Implicit Challenge** — An implicit challenge is not specifically added to the game, but is an emergent feature of the game itself. *Explicit Challenge* is intentional and immediate.
21. **D. Purely luck-based outcomes** — Purely luck-based games are noted as the exception where skill doesn't determine the winner, NOT a requirement for balance. The other three are among the 5 balance requirements.
22. **B. Lack of trivial decisions** — Requiring players to make only important decisions, even in micromanagement games, is Lack of trivial decisions. *Lack of stagnation* prevents getting stuck.
23. **B. Lack of stagnation** — Players should never get stuck with no way to go on. A bug that blocks all progress directly violates this requirement.
